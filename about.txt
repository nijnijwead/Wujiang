Wujiang: The Places We Hold Dear

This project documents everyday life in Wujiang, one of the tens of thousands of county towns in China, through participant photography, documentary photography, and ethnographic fieldwork. It aims at bringing together different ways of seeing the same place: what its young residents choose to record, what I notice through my own camera, and what emerges through conversations and time spent together.

What Makes Us Happy?

In this section, I invited several local young people to use cameras to document things that made them happy. Rather than directing them toward particular places or subjects, I gave them one simple instruction: record the things that make you happy.

Their choices became a way into their everyday lives—into the places they frequent, the things they value, and the moments they consider worth preserving. These photographs are not illustrations of my observations, nor do they offer a complete account of each photographer's life. They are personal perspectives, shaped by what each person chose to notice and share. Presented alongside their words and my field notes, they create a space for different interpretations of Wujiang to coexist.

What Remains?

From an outsider's perspective, my own photographs explore the relationship between people and the environments they inhabit. I photograph landscapes, buildings, interiors, portraits of local residents, and smaller details of everyday life. From an aesthetic perspective, the county town brings me infinite nostalgia and a sense of childhood fever dreams. 

I am drawn to spaces that bear traces of previous activity, gathering places that remain in use, and the contrasts between physical surroundings and the lives unfolding within them. Rather than presenting Wujiang as simply declining or thriving, I hope to document its contradictions and complexities, leaving room for the place to exceed my own expectations.
