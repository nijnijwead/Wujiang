A Future Between Cities

This project documents the everyday life in Wujiang, one of the tens of thousands of county towns in China, through participant photography, documentary photography, and ethnographic fieldwork. It aims at presenting Wujiang through an interactive perspective: combining what its young residents chose to record, what I (an outside researcher) recorded, and what emerged through conversations together.

The Places We Hold Dear

I asked several local young people to document Wujiang with disposable cameras. Rather than directing them toward particular places or subjects, I gave them one simple instruction: “record the things and people that you hold dear.”

These imageries offer a passage into their everyday lives—the places they frequent, the things they value, and the moments they consider worth preserving. These photographs are presented alongside their words and my field notes, to create a space for different interpretations of Wujiang centered on the younger local generation’s personal experiences.

What Remains?

My own photographs explore the relationship between people and the environments they inhabit, focusing on landscapes, buildings, interiors, portraits of local residents, and smaller details of everyday life.

I hope to capture spaces that bear traces of previous activity, specifically the juxtaposition of physical surroundings and the lives unfolding within them. Rather than presenting Wujiang as simply declining or thriving, I hope to document its contradictions and complexities, leaving room for the place to exceed my own expectations.
