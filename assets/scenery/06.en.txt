The Alive

“The Alive” is not synonymous with living. It confronts people and things that are real and broken, yet still exist and still live: without the conditions for a good life, they still maintain their daily lives through repair, making do, detours, and endurance.

Here, I am in fact speaking with affirmation, but the Chinese term for merely surviving twists that affirmation: it is not about living vigorously, only about not having been eliminated yet. It does not praise resilience, nor does it romanticize hardship. It simply acknowledges that brokenness is not the end, but material for continuing to live—and continuing to live is itself a concrete and tenacious fact.
