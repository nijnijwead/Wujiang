The Ethereal Nights

The new moon is the name of this series. Since there is a new one, it naturally suggests an old moon as well. The old moon—the moon the world gives to the earth—is the light nature lends to Wujiang. Soft and ambiguous, it keeps a veil over the town and leaves outsiders a place where they can safely be lyrical. It makes decay feel like nostalgia, and strangeness feel like mystery.

The new moon that takes over the night, however, is the flash in my hand. At night, the flash imposes a hierarchy on everything ordinary. At night, the flash leaves everything nowhere to hide. As a photographer, I control this new moon; I decide what is worth recording and what is worth illuminating. All of this is built on my aesthetics, so its distortion is a distortion seen from my perspective. In my eyes, everything ordinary seems unfamiliar and special; under the light of the old moon, it may simply be ordinary.
