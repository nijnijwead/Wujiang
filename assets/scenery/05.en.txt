The Lucid Days

“The Lucid Days” is not about brightness, but about overexposure and heat. It drags the county town out of the ambiguity of night, fading colors, shortening shadows, and stripping everything of cover.

The harsh sun emphasizes the cost of clarity: the more clearly you see, the fewer places there are to hide. If the new moon is the flash at night, the harsh sun is a continuous exposure without a shutter—a light like an audit that turns life into something that can be inspected, compared, and proven. Stripped of that melancholic literary rhetoric, everything is shown truthfully, which instead gives these things a more subtle meaning.
