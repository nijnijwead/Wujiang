The Interior

“The Interior” photographs the clutter of indoor spaces. Crowded, stacked, temporarily pieced-together things each perform their own function, like organs. The house is small, dilapidated, and messy, yet it still works—a sparrow may be small, but it has all its vital organs.

To outsiders, the mess looks like disorder, but in fact it is a map only its residents can read. People can move naturally through these puzzle-like rooms because their bodies have long remembered where each thing is. Familiarity turns narrowness into order, and makes brokenness no longer mean an inability to live. This series is concerned with how people use space as their own body. The house is their most intimate interior, and a place of belonging they do not need to explain to the outside.
