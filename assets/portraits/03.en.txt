Chicken-Wing Bro is JA's friend. I met him while fishing, and he's also in seventh grade.
JA brought him along to fish with me by the river. He was curious about me too. Unlike JA, who was always teasing me, he looked out for me a little more and spoke up for me.
There's a story behind his name. The third or fourth time we played basketball together, we ended up on opposing teams. He kept coming over to guard me, even though I was at least 20 centimeters taller. When we were well ahead, he secretly pulled a pack of chicken wings out of his pocket, the kind from dollar snack shops, and slipped it into my hand.
That may have been one of the most touching moments I had in Wujiang. His hands were small; he cupped the packet of chicken wings in both of them, pressed it firmly into my palm, then smiled and told me not to tell anyone.
When I got back to the hotel, I tried the chicken wings. Maybe because my expectations were low, or maybe because of the weight of the gesture, they tasted surprisingly good.

Later, when I gave Chicken-Wing Bro the camera, he accepted it with the same grin he wore for everything.
His photos took him about a week, and he was still grinning when he gave the camera back.
