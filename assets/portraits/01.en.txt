JA is one of the kids I've known longest. We met the first time I went to the basketball court. After we finished playing, he asked if I had any water and whether I could get him some. Once I agreed, he asked if he could have a soft drink instead. When I said yes to that, he asked whether it could be milk tea. In the end I ordered two cups of Mixue (one of the most affordable milk tea brands)(I nearly went with Guming, a more average priced one, but he teased me: "You're not seriously going to buy me Guming, are you?"), and just like that, I had bought myself an "informant."
When I met him, JA was about to enter sixth grade. Now he's in seventh grade.
My trips to town were mostly about meeting up with him to fish, play basketball, and occasionally shoot pool. Because I was good at basketball, he kept asking me to coach him. Eventually he brought a bunch of his friends along to practice with me, and I gradually became a coach of sorts.

I gave him the camera around our fifth or sixth meeting, when I was about to head back to Nanjing. We were having noodles at a noodle shop. At first he was dead set against it and wouldn't take it at all.
I asked him why. "What's there to photograph?" he said. He had a point since his WeChat Moments had never featured a photo he'd taken, only game screenshots and reposted videos.
I asked why he didn't at least give it a try. He was starting to sound impatient. "Why bother taking photos? It's such a hassle."
"To document life," I said.
"Nah. I'm not doing it."
I couldn't really force him, so I dropped it.
Later, while we were eating, I caught him zoning out and snapped a photo of him with the flash (01). Grinning, he snatched the camera from my hands and retaliated by flashing me right back (02). It was the first photo he ever took with the camera.
After that, he seemed to love it, as if he'd realized the task wasn't so scary after all.
Of all the young people, he finished fastest. When I asked about his progress on the third day, he said he was already done. I worried he was just messing around, but he swore that everything he'd shot was something that made him happy. So, with a knot in my stomach, I took the film to be developed.
